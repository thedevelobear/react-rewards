# 0067. Fireworks climb in closed form and burst as peonies, willows and crackles

- **Status:** Proposed
- **Date:** 2026-10-24

- **The volley.** `count` is rockets, launched from one point: the first at once, the rest within 0.45 s, each in its own share of a fan `spread` wide around `angle`, at 0.9 to 1.1 times `velocity`.
- **The climb.** A rocket is placed on its closed-form flight under `drag` and `gravity`, not pushed by its velocity. It spirals up to `spiral` px around that path, on a helix that turns every 64 px climbed, and lays a gold trail spark every 7 px. It bursts just before it would stall, at 0.9 of the time a rocket fired straight up at its speed takes to stop rising, `ln(1 + speed·drag/gravity) / drag`. Without gravity it bursts once drag has taken 90% of its speed, at `ln 10 / drag`.
- **The shells.** Each bursts as a peony, coloured stars that droop and fade; a willow, gold stars (`#FFA944`) whose long trails hang, with half of `stars`; or a crackle, coloured stars that flicker, go dark and pop into six white-gold sparks each, with three quarters of `stars`. Each shell flashes for 0.2 s. The weights `peony`, `willow` and `crackle` pick the kind, and all 0 means peony.
- **Sampling.** The volley's shared draws come from `createRng(rng.seed ^ salt)` in `spawn`: where the kinds, the colours and the fan start. Rocket `index` takes the next share of the weights and the next colour, so at 1:1:1 a 3-rocket volley shows one of each kind. The rocket's head is drawn in its shell's colour.
- **Children.** Trail sparks, stars, sparks and flashes are placed and aged at the moment they were made within the step: rocket trails by Newton's method on the climb's closed form, willow trails by arc length along the star's path, stars and sparks by the time since the burst or pop. Each takes its randomness from a seed hashed from its parent's seed, not from `ctx.rng`.
- **Drag.** `drag` acts on the climb only. Stars have a fixed drag per kind (peony 4, willow 3, crackle 4.5 per second), with speeds set so they reach `radius` (0.8 of it for willows, 0.85 for crackles). Crackle sparks reach up to 0.16 × `radius`.
- **Lifetime.** Every life is capped by the show's time left, so everything is gone by `lifetime`. A late rocket launches early enough to burst at least 1 s before the end, where the show is long enough. When the show is shorter than a climb, every rocket is a dud: it launches at once and fades out over the last 0.2 s without bursting.
- **Look.** The default colours are red, green, blue, sodium gold (`#FFD84A`) and white, with the additive blend. Glows are radial gradients drawn in each sprite's `draw`, from the colour as the canvas reads it back. A colour it writes another way, such as `oklch()`, is drawn as a plain dot. The helpers live beside `effect.ts` in `flight.ts`, `glow.ts` and `noise.ts`.
- **Defaults.** `count` 3, `size` 5, `spread` 60, `velocity` 1200, `drag` 2.5, `gravity` 250, `spiral` 5, `stars` 40, `radius` 120, weights 1, 1 and 1, and `lifetime` 3500, so a show matches confetti's scale: the shells top out about 467 px up and spread about 278 px to either side, where confetti's highest tenth of pieces tops out at 462 px and it spreads 281 px. The apex is 377.4 px at 1.026 s. Shells burst at 0.923 s, 376.0 px up (332.0 to 420.3 px across launch speeds). A rocket peaks at about 126 live particles.

**Why:** Real fireworks rise on a thin trail, slow down and burst near the top, and peonies, willows and crackles are classic shells that are easy to tell apart. The closed form makes the burst height exact. A child made partway through a step and stepped as if it were made at the step's end would bunch trails by frame rate, and `ctx.rng` is drawn from as often as there are steps, so placing children within the step and seeding them from their parents keeps a seeded show the same particle for particle at any frame rate: within 0.25 px of 144 Hz at 30 Hz. Systematic sampling keeps a small volley varied. The defaults match confetti's scale, because the effects share one scale, so swapping effects keeps a reward the same size.

**Consequences:** `useReward + firework` is 9.91 kB minified and gzipped, the most of any effect. White shells and crackle sparks are faint on light pages, and the site says the effect looks best on dark backgrounds. The Stress test page and `pnpm bench` ask for one rocket per 130 particles, so the firework's particle counts there are approximate. The two techniques for children are in `docs/guides/adding-an-effect.md` and the effect-creator agent.
